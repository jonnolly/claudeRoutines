# Tip 001 — 2026-11-12
## Put a *second, blind* Claude between Alex's summary and your "push OK"

**Time to try: ~5 min on your next release. Why today: Alex went live on 31 Oct, so the habit is cheap to form now and expensive to retrofit later.**

### The gap
Alex's rules say nothing reaches the server, a machine or production without your OK — so you are the last gate on every push and every firmware release. But habit #6 is *"read my summary"*: Alex tells you what it ran, what it couldn't check, and what's open. That means **the author is grading its own homework, and your gate is only as good as its self-report.** For firmware you can't bench-test on every change, that's where the expensive mistakes live.

### The move
Before you say "push", open a **separate** Claude session and give it *only* the goal and the diff. Not Alex's summary, not Alex's reasoning, and don't mention that an agent wrote it.

```powershell
git diff master...HEAD > C:\Code\review.diff
```

Then, in the fresh session:

> Review this diff for correctness only. The intended change was: `<one line>`.
> Hunt specifically for: off-by-one on pack/slot indices, CAN id collisions or wrong bus,
> signedness and width bugs, uninitialised or stale state across calls, error paths that
> return success, and tests that assert the implementation instead of the behaviour.
> Report only defects you can give me a concrete failing input for. If you find none, say so.

If your harness has `/code-review`, that one command does it.

### Why this specific shape
The value is **not** delegation — it's that the reviewer hasn't seen the author's reasoning. Anthropic's own best-practices page makes exactly this point: a second session is the easiest independent reviewer, and its worth comes from *not* having been part of the first conversation. Every 2026 practitioner write-up converges on the same two-agent pattern (A implements, B reviews correctness/edge cases/missed tests). The failure mode to avoid is handing the reviewer your conclusion — an agent given a hypothesis tends to return it confirmed. So: diff in, no narrative.

### Make it stick (only once it's earned its keep)
After it catches its first real bug, add a step to the **`release` skill in `NE_ALEX`**: *"independent review of the diff, in a session that has not seen the implementation, before push."* Then it survives you forgetting, and Rogier gets it for free. That's the compounding half — one correction written into `NE_ALEX` is worth more than ten you make in a session that ends.
